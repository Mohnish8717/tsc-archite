[SEGMENT: TECH & TUTORIAL CREATORS — HIGH FOLLOWER COUNT]

MKBHD (Marques Brownlee, ~15M subscribers at time of announcement):
The dislike bar is a one-glanceable quality indicator. If I search for how to
fix a blue screen error and I see a video has a 95% like ratio vs one with a
10% like ratio, I instantly know which one is likely to help me. Removing the
public number doesn't help the creator — I can still see it in Studio. It only
removes information from the viewer. Viewers are the ones being hurt here.

Linus Sebastian (Linus Tech Tips, ~14M subscribers, WAN Show):
Let's think about who is actually protected by this. Small creators get 50 views
and maybe 3 dislikes. The ratio is irrelevant at that scale — nobody organizes
a dislike campaign against a channel with 200 subscribers. Who actually gets
dislike-bombed in a way that appears on the news? It's channels large enough to
attract organized opposition. And what's the most disliked video in the history
of the platform? YouTube Rewind 2018. Made by YouTube. For advertisers. The
people who benefit from hiding the dislike bar are the people who own the platform
and the brands who buy ads on it. That's not small creators.

PewDiePie (Felix Kjellberg, ~111M subscribers):
A like button without a dislike button is just an engagement metric. It tells you
nothing about whether the content is actually good. You might as well just show
view count. The dislike ratio was the only meaningful quality signal the viewer had.

[SEGMENT: EDUCATIONAL & DIY CREATORS]

Creator posting DIY electrical tutorial:
My channel teaches home electrical work. If I make a mistake in a video showing
how to wire an outlet, the electricians in my comments will dislike it immediately
and leave corrections. That dislike ratio is a WARNING to other viewers that
something is wrong before they start following instructions. Removing it is
genuinely dangerous for instructional content.

Programming tutorial creator (r/learnprogramming thread):
I teach Python and React. Libraries change constantly. When a tutorial becomes
outdated because a framework releases a breaking change, the community dislikes
it to warn beginners. New developers searching for tutorials cannot tell the
difference between a correct current tutorial and a deprecated one without the
dislike ratio. This will result in beginners wasting hours following broken code.

Medical content creator:
I create evidence-based health content. The dislike button was the fastest way
the medical community could signal to viewers that a video contained dangerous
health misinformation. Anti-vaccine content, dangerous supplement advice, fake
COVID cures — all relied on the dislike ratio as the first-line community warning.
Hiding it doesn't remove the videos. It removes the warning label.

[SEGMENT: DEVELOPER COMMUNITY — API DEPRECATION IMPACT]

Stack Overflow thread (Nov-Dec 2021):
The YouTube Data API v3 `dislikeCount` field is being deprecated for unauthenticated
requests on December 13, 2021. Every app that called `videos.list` and rendered
that field just broke. The exemption process requires proving you won't display
the data publicly — which means extensions like SponsorBlock integrations that
showed ratios are effectively killed. No migration path was provided.

GitHub issue thread on youtube-dl / yt-dlp:
Affected: any tool parsing video metadata that included dislike ratio for filtering
or sorting. Workarounds require either web scraping (fragile, violates ToS) or
using the Return YouTube Dislike API which estimates based on crowdsourced data
from extension users — not remotely as accurate as the official count.

Hacker News top comment (Nov 10, 2021):
This is not a UI change. This is an API deprecation. YouTube deliberately made
it impossible to build a workaround using the official API. The exemption process
is a fig leaf — the approval threshold requires that you demonstrably do NOT show
the data to end users, which defeats the entire purpose of why a developer would
want the data. They didn't just remove a number from the UI. They designed the
removal to be uncircumventable.

[SEGMENT: CONTENT RESEARCHERS & MISINFORMATION ANALYSTS]

Academic researcher (published commentary, Media Studies):
The public dislike ratio functioned as a distributed content quality assessment
mechanism. On a platform processing 500 hours of uploaded content per minute,
no moderation team can review everything. Community signal — including dislikes —
served as a scalable first-pass filter. Removing public visibility shifts
quality assessment entirely to the algorithmic recommendation system, which has
well-documented problems with surfacing engaging but inaccurate content.

Misinformation researcher:
The dislike ratio correlated measurably with fact-checker flags on health
misinformation content. Videos with >30% dislike ratio were 4x more likely to
contain disputed medical claims. That's a rough proxy, but it's a proxy that
required no moderation resources and updated in real time. That signal is now
invisible to viewers.

[SEGMENT: PROFESSIONAL VIDEO PRODUCERS & AGENCIES]

Brand agency video producer:
My clients run corporate YouTube channels. The dislike bar was actually useful
for us — it showed us when a campaign was landing poorly in real time, before
the full analytics data came in. Now I have to wait for Studio data to understand
immediate community reaction. The dislike ratio was the fastest feedback signal
available. Removing it slows our ability to react to a poorly received video.
